# Step 3 - PaySim cannot validate a transfer model

The transfer rail trained and scored **PR-AUC 1.0000**. That is not a result. It
is the simulator being visible through the data, and the number is being
recorded here as a finding rather than a performance figure.

## What gave it away

Three things, in order:

1. A **logistic regression** scored PR-AUC 0.9986.
2. LightGBM early-stopped after **13 rounds**.
3. LightGBM then scored **1.0000** exactly.

Any one of those is a leakage signature. Together they are conclusive.

## The mechanism

One derived feature - "the amount equals the sender's entire balance, to the
cent":

| | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Among fraud | **97.82%** (8,034 of 8,213) |
| Among legitimate | **0.00%** (0 of 2,762,196) |

Zero. Not rare - **zero out of 2.76 million**.

A three-line rule with no model at all:

```python
takes_everything = abs(amount - oldbalanceOrg) < 0.01
never_credited   = (oldbalanceDest == 0) & (newbalanceDest == 0)
flag = takes_everything & never_credited
```

flags 3,928 rows, catches **47.8% of all fraud at 100% precision, zero false
positives**.

## Why this invalidates the rail rather than being a great feature

PaySim generates fraud by draining an account to the exact cent, and its
legitimate traffic never does so. Real payment traffic does this constantly:
paying rent with the last of a balance, closing an account, moving savings
across. A real population producing **zero** such transactions in 2.76 million
is not plausible.

The model is not learning fraud. It is learning the simulator's fraud-generation
routine.

## Removing the artifacts does not rescue it

Dropping every origin-balance feature (`takes_everything`, `orig_reconciles`,
`error_balance_orig`, `oldbalanceOrg`, `newbalanceOrig`, `amount_over_balance`,
`orig_emptied`) still leaves PR-AUC **0.7174**, with 59.4% of fraud caught at
91.3% precision on a 0.77% base rate.

That remains implausible, and the reason is the same: the destination-balance
columns are generated by the same routine. `dest_still_empty` and
`error_balance_dest` encode the simulator's behaviour just as directly.

Contamination is not in a feature. It is in the dataset.

## Consequence

**No transfer-rail performance number should be reported from PaySim.** The
pipeline code is sound and reusable - the router, the chronological split, the
metrics - but the numbers it produces on this source measure a simulator.

This is the empirical form of the circularity warning the previous project
recorded in `DATA_SOURCES.md`. It was a caution there; it is a measurement here.

## Options

1. **Ship the pipeline, report no accuracy.** Honest, and leaves the rail
   unvalidated until real telemetry exists.
2. **Find a real transfer dataset.** Nothing public is known to cover P2P push
   payments with labels.
3. **Use PaySim only as a load and latency harness**, which is what synthetic
   data is legitimately good for.

Recommended: 1 and 3. The card rail (IEEE-CIS) is real transaction data and its
numbers stand; the transfer rail should not borrow credibility from a
simulator.
