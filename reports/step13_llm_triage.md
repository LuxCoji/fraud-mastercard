# Step 13 - LLM consensus triage: rejected

The wire rail queues cases at ~12% precision overall, and 60% in the top 40. The
plan was a panel of independent LLM reviews with a majority vote - the pattern
Visa's Vulnerability Agentic Harness uses for false-positive filtering - to raise
that before an investigator sees it.

Built, measured, **rejected**.

## The result

Top 40 cases, panel of 3 at temperature 0.7, `openai/gpt-oss-120b` via Groq:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline precision | 60% (24 real of 40) |
| Precision after escalation filter | **60%** |
| **Real cases the panel did not escalate** | **12 of 24** |

**No precision gain, and half the real laundering dropped.** A filter that
raises precision by discarding real cases has made the system worse; this one
did not even raise precision.

## Where it fails, precisely

| Panel verdict | Cases | Real | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "laundering" | 5 | 4 | **80%** |
| "unclear" | 8 | 6 | 75% |
| "legitimate" | 26 | 13 | **50%** |

Its positive verdict is genuinely informative - 80% against a 60% baseline. Its
**negative verdict is a coin flip**, and that is the one a filter acts on. The
panel can recognise laundering when it sees it and cannot recognise its absence.

## Reordering the queue does not help either

The obvious salvage was to use the panel for ranking rather than filtering -
promote what it calls laundering, never demote anything. Measured:

| | Model order | Panel-reordered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| top 5 | 80% | 80% |
| top 10 | **90%** | 80% |
| top 13 | **85%** | 77% |
| top 20 | 80% | 80% |

**The model's own ranking is already better.** The panel adds nothing it does
not already know, and at top-10 it actively hurts.

## Why, honestly

Two reasons, and the first is the more interesting:

**The model already used the structure.** The panel reads traced flows and
account counts - but `graph_patterns.py` fed cycle counts, fan widths and
pass-through ratios into the model as features. The LLM is reasoning over
evidence the model has already priced in, which is the same mistake the
cross-border threshold made in step 11. **A second opinion on the same evidence
is not a second opinion.**

**The source is synthetic.** IBM AML's laundering is generated by a simulator,
so its structures need not resemble what an LLM's real-world AML knowledge
recognises. When the panel says "a single fan-in of modest amounts is typical
merchant settlement", that is correct about real banking and wrong about this
dataset. The failure may therefore be partly a property of the data rather than
of the approach - but it cannot be distinguished without real data, so it cannot
be claimed as a defence.

## A bug worth recording

The first run reported **109 of 120 calls failed**, and every failure became an
"unclear" vote. The panel therefore reported *unanimous agreement at 100%* on 33
of 40 cases while having asked nothing at all.

That is the dangerous shape: the queue looked triaged, the confidence looked
high, and the output was noise. Groq throttles at twelve concurrent requests -
three panel members times four concurrent cases - and the retry logic did not
exist.

Fixed with exponential backoff honouring `Retry-After`, and by treating a failed
call as **not an opinion** rather than as an "unclear" vote. A panel where every
member failed now returns `unreviewed` and escalates by default, which is the
safe direction.

## What is kept

`src/policy/triage.py` remains, unwired. Two things make it worth keeping:

- Its **positive** verdict is 80% accurate, which is a usable signal if a future
  use needs one - just not for filtering.
- It writes a one-sentence explanation per case ("the flow forms a multi-hop
  cycle that returns money to its origin"), which is exactly the case-summary
  text an investigator dashboard needs. **Explaining a case is a job the LLM does
  well; deciding one is not.**

That is the honest split, and it is where step 5 of the plan should have aimed:
LLM as narrator, not as judge.
